Our mission

Start with what they love.

A kid who says they "aren't into" coding or AI will happily argue for an hour about whether a ball was in or out. That curiosity is already there. It just needs a door.

The Nush Project builds those doors. We take the sports kids already care about and turn them into a way in: to technology, to learning, and to new interests. The goal isn't to make athletes. It's to show kids how much more they can be.

A Nush Project · AI Literacy

AI Ref: Train Your Own AI Referee

A free game that teaches kids how AI really learns, no experience needed.

Can you teach an AI to make a fair call? In AI Ref, kids become AI trainers: they teach a friendly robot referee, Ref-Bot, to judge whether a badminton shot lands IN or OUT. Instead of just using AI, they build it, showing Ref-Bot examples, testing what it learned, and fixing it when it gets things wrong.

Across five quick levels they discover how AI learns from examples, why it needs diverse data to stay fair, and why AI still needs people to check it. Built to run on low-cost phones and low-bandwidth connections, in English and Hindi, with a no-technology classroom version for schools without devices.

Why "Nush"

Started by a player.

The Nush Project was founded by Anusha Schonfeld, a basketball player who also writes code. Sport was never only about the game. It was a way into other things.

Everything here, from the AI projects to the classroom sessions, grew out of that idea. It's run by young people, for young people, and it's just getting started.
