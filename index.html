Our mission

Keep girls in the game.

Too many girls stop playing sport in their teens. Not because they lose interest in the game, but because they lose the team around it — the friends, the coaches, the sense that there's a place for them.

The Nush Project exists to build that place. We connect girls through competition, mentor younger players in classrooms, and use technology — including AI — to make sport fairer, smarter and more open to everyone.

What we do

Three ways in. One team.

01

On the court — UK50

Competition is where friendships get forged. Through UK50 basketball we bring girls from different teams and schools onto the same court, and keep the stats, the stories and the community going long after the final whistle.

  • Tournament play & team building
  • Box scores and player stats
  • Connecting players across clubs
02

In the classroom

Older players teaching younger ones. We go into classrooms to run sessions with younger students — the fundamentals of the game, what being a good teammate looks like, and the confidence to show up and play.

  • Student-led sessions for younger kids
  • Skills, teamwork & confidence
  • Mentors who look like them
03

In code — AI Ref

Sport and technology belong together. Our AI Ref project explores how artificial intelligence can help officiate games more consistently — and shows girls that they can build the tech behind the sport, not just play it.

  • The AI Ref project
  • Coding with AI for young athletes
  • Sport as a way into STEM

How it connects

Play. Teach. Build. Repeat.

  1. PlayGirls meet through competition and become a team.
  2. TeachThey take what they've learned into classrooms for the next generation.
  3. BuildThey use code and AI to make the game better for everyone.

Why "Nush"

Started by a player.

The Nush Project was founded by Anusha Schonfeld, a basketball player who also writes code. It started with a simple observation: the girls who stuck with sport were the ones who had each other.

Everything here — the tournaments, the classroom sessions, the AI projects — grew out of that. It's run by young people, for young people, and it's just getting started.
